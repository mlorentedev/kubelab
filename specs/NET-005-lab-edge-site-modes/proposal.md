# NET-005: Portable lab edge with a per-site uplink

## Why

<!-- from issue #2661: NET-005: portable lab edge — RPi4 as the network edge with a per-site uplink -->

The homelab has been offline since the operator moved out of the home network: its only uplink was the home router's LAN, and the temporary site offers an open Wi-Fi behind a captive portal that authorizes one MAC address at a time. Every site change so far meant hand-editing the RPi4's netplan (a heredoc in `docs/runbooks/rpi4-bootstrap.md` with an Imager-extracted PSK), so the uplink is not in the SSOT and a move is manual and unreproducible. The next move, to a new ISP router in Spain in about four weeks, must be a one-block profile change, and the personal devices (phones, tablet, laptops, a work laptop on video calls) need the same trusted network behind Pi-hole at every site.

## What

After this change:

- **The lab owns its edge; only the uplink changes per site.** `networking.edge.uplink` in `common.yaml` describes the current site's uplink: its kind (open or PSK Wi-Fi through the USB Wi-Fi adapter, or Ethernet through the USB Ethernet adapter), the interface it binds, and the site's regulatory domain and shaping bandwidth. Anything that identifies a site (the uplink SSID, any BSSID or portal host, and the lab Wi-Fi SSID from now on) lives in SOPS beside the PSKs, because this repository is public and an ISP router's default SSID locates a home. So does the uplink adapter's MAC, the key to the paid access: the profile binds a stable interface name the role assigns, never the kernel's `wlx<mac>` name. The `gateway` role renders the RPi4's uplink network config, NAT and firewall from it. Moving site edits that block and re-runs provisioning; nothing else changes.
- **The RPi4 broadcasts the lab Wi-Fi.** Its built-in radio is a 2.4 GHz access point (ADR-075 D2) for `networking.edge.lab_wifi`, under the home network's SSID so saved devices rejoin on their own. It serves DHCP on the home network's CIDR, from the home router's address, with Pi-hole as DNS and NAT to the uplink. Reusing the CIDR keeps every static or reserved client, `networking.nodes.rpi3.lan_ip` and the `rpi3-lan` alias valid (ADR-075 D1). Its PSK lives in SOPS. rpi3 rejoins it and keeps its reserved address, so Uptime Kuma comes back on the tailnet.
- **The homelab LAN is unchanged and isolated, and its DHCP works again.** `eth0` keeps `networking.lan_cidr`, NAT and the Tailscale subnet route. The baseline of 2026-10-25 found its DHCP dead: dnsmasq listens on `eth0:67`, but ufw's default-deny input drops every request (27 dropped in the first five minutes after boot, and the operator's laptop got no lease). The lab Wi-Fi's DHCP needs the same allowance. Nothing is forwarded between the lab Wi-Fi and the homelab LAN; devices reach the homelab over the tailnet, as they did at home.
- **The edge trusts nothing on the uplink.** Today the RPi4 forwards with ufw's `DEFAULT_FORWARD_POLICY="ACCEPT"`, and ufw allows 22, 80, 443 and 41641 on every interface, which was tolerable behind a home router and is not on a network shared with strangers. ufw stays, since `base_system` owns it on every node, and it governs what the host itself accepts. It does not govern forwarded or DNAT'd traffic: Tailscale's and Docker's chains accept that traffic before ufw's rules apply. So the gateway's `inet kubelab` table is the only control for forwarded traffic, and its drops must be complete on their own. A drop in any nftables base chain is final, whatever the order (ADR-075 D3). The uplink's drops load before the uplink first comes up, in the same run. After this change:
  - Nothing arriving on the uplink is forwarded unless it belongs to a connection the inside opened. That covers routed traffic to the homelab LAN and the lab Wi-Fi, and Docker-published ports (Pi-hole DNS and web, CoreDNS), whose DNAT bypasses ufw (#959).
  - The host accepts no new connection on the uplink. SSH stays on the homelab LAN, the lab Wi-Fi and the tailnet.
  - The uplink's DHCP lease supplies only an address and a default gateway. Classless static routes (option 121, the TunnelVision class), DNS servers, NTP, domains and hostname are ignored. In systemd-networkd 255 terms this is `UseRoutes=no` plus a drop-in with `UseGateway=yes`:
    - netplan's `use-routes: false` also drops the default gateway, because `UseGateway` defaults to `UseRoutes`;
    - netplan exposes no `use-gateway`;
    - with `UseRoutes=no`, option 3 is honoured even when the server sends option 121 (read in `networkd-dhcp4.c`, v255).
  - IPv6 is off on the uplink. Reverse-path filtering is strict on the uplink and loose elsewhere, because the tailnet subnet route is asymmetric; Tailscale supports strict filtering from 1.96.2, and the role asserts that floor. ARP sysctls keep internal addresses off the uplink, and ICMP redirects and source routing are refused.
  - The resolver is pinned by the role. CAKE shapes the uplink with per-host fairness in both directions, ingress through an IFB device, because a video call suffers from the download queue as much as from the upload.
  - The lab Wi-Fi uses WPA2 with CCMP only and no WPS. Management-frame protection stays off: AP-mode MFP fails on the Pi 4's radio through firmware 7.45.251 (raspberrypi/linux#3619), and the RPi4 runs 7.45.234. WPA3 stays off too, because SAE crashes this firmware (raspberrypi/linux#7528).
- **An uplink that collides with an internal CIDR is reported.** A lease inside `networking.lan_cidr`, the lab Wi-Fi CIDR or the tailnet CIDR raises an operator alert instead of silently breaking routes.
- **A captive portal needs no screen on the RPi4, and its return is announced.** Login, the paid upgrade and the 5-day renewal are done from any client on the lab Wi-Fi, since the portal sees only the uplink's MAC. The paid upgrade is sold by time period (hour, day, week or month), not per stay, so it expires too. A portal that takes the uplink back silences the edge: HTTPS and the tailnet are both blocked, so the edge cannot report it. Prod reports it instead (next item). Locally, the edge tells a portal from a dead uplink with a connectivity check, which a Make target reads from any laptop on the lab Wi-Fi. If the site answers DNS before login only from its own resolver, a bounded login mode forwards the portal's domains to that resolver and restarts Pi-hole on exit (ADR-075 D3). The runbook covers both site kinds and the switch between them.
- **Prod notices when the edge or rpi3 goes silent.** A prod workload probes the RPi4 and rpi3 over the tailnet on a schedule, and logs one line per target on every run, with the result as a value. Vector ships the lines to Loki, and a Grafana rule alerts through the ADR-044 fabric on a failed target; a second rule alerts when no lines arrive, the pattern of `r2-backup-watcher` and OBS-015. A target that is missing from the logs instead of failing would read as resolved: Grafana routes an instance whose series disappears as `MissingSeries`, resolved. That one signal covers a dead uplink, a portal that is back, a dead RPi4, and an Uptime Kuma that went silent with it (ADR-075 D6).
- **The RPi4 becomes always-on, and recovers what it can by itself.** It is now the edge for rpi3 and every personal device, so `common.yaml` reclassifies it and ADR-075 amends ADR-028's placement. Its radio's firmware can halt and needs a reboot (raspberrypi/firmware#1403), so the role arms the hardware watchdog and a liveness timer that reloads the driver, then reboots (ADR-075 D5).

## Out of scope

- The guest network (NET-004, #2363).
- Renumbering the homelab LAN out of `172.16.0.0/12`. The temporary site's `172.16.0.0/23` overlaps it today; this change detects the collision (AC7), and the renumbering is NET-006 (#2662).
- A dedicated access point for coverage at the next site, and per-client Pi-hole policy (such as a non-blocking group for the work laptop).
- Rescheduling `node_backup` for a slow uplink, unless the shaping measurement in AC8 shows it is needed.
- Scripting a portal's login (decided by the operator, 2026-10-25). The temporary site's free tier has no CAPTCHA or token and could be scripted, but:
  - the code would serve one hotel's app;
  - it would keep the guest's name, room and email in SOPS;
  - it could only be validated against server-side checks with a live capture.

  Detection and the alert are generic and in scope; the login itself stays a declared human step (AC11).

## Risks / open questions

- **[RESOLVED 2026-10-25, baseline] Auto-join needs the same SSID, PSK and a compatible security mode.** The RPi4's own saved network (written from the same Imager settings as rpi3's) is the plain SSID, not a `-5G` variant, with a PSK. Clients save a network by SSID and security, not by band, and a profile saved from a WPA2/WPA3 transition network accepts WPA2-PSK, so the AP runs WPA2-PSK with CCMP on 2.4 GHz (ADR-075 D2). A device that saved a WPA3-only profile re-enters the PSK once.
- **[RESOLVED 2026-10-25, from the repo's own evidence] DNS exposure is controlled by the forward rule, not by bind addresses.** Pi-hole must answer on the homelab LAN, the lab Wi-Fi and the tailnet address (Headscale split DNS points at it). Publishing on the tailnet address is the failure recorded in `infra/ansible/roles/coredns/templates/docker-compose.yml.j2`: on 2026-08-22, Docker restored the containers before tailscaled had the address, the bind failed, and the whole port publication went with it, for ninety minutes of staging DNS. So the publication stays as it is and the uplink's default-drop forward chain is the control. A drop in any nftables base chain is final, whatever Docker's own chains accept.
- **[RESOLVED 2026-10-25, ADR-075 D6] The alert channel.** Uptime Kuma sits behind the same edge, so it can report a colliding lease (the uplink still works) but not a dead uplink. The two origins are:
  - from the edge, a collision fails a unit, and `node_notify` posts to prod n8n;
  - from prod, the probe of AC12.

  The operator brought the probe into scope on 2026-10-25, because without it nothing reports a portal that is back.
- **The provisioning run can cut its own session.** The inventory reaches the RPi4 at its tailnet address, and the tailnet rides the uplink this change reconfigures. Edge changes therefore run over the homelab LAN (`lan_ip`) from the operator's laptop on the switch, never over the tailnet, and the runbook says so.
- **The tailnet must come up through each uplink.** ADR-013 records Headscale's Noise protocol dying behind a TLS-inspecting firewall; a hotel or ISP network is neither of the two networks it was validated on. AC1 checks the session per uplink kind; the fallback is ADR-052's public bastion path. Re-authentication is not needed: on 2026-10-25, Headscale listed rpi4's and rpi3's node keys with no expiry, so both rejoin once they have a route out.
- **The public Docker address pool now affects every device.** Docker's pool is `172.80.0.0/12`, public address space (#2136, open). Its bridges on the RPi4 (`172.80.0.0/24` to `172.80.3.0/24` in the baseline) were a local quirk; as the edge, the RPi4 routes every lab Wi-Fi client's traffic for those public addresses into its own bridges. Not fixed here; #2136 gains this consequence.
- **Lockout on the only edge.** The RPi4 is now the only path to the internet and to the homelab. `eth0` input stays open to the homelab LAN, so the operator's laptop on the homelab switch (USB Ethernet, `ssh 172.16.1.1`) is the recovery path, and every nftables change is checked with `nft -c` before it loads. A read-only baseline of the RPi4 is captured before the first provision.
- **Shared fate (ADR-028).** rpi3's monitoring path is no longer independent of the homelab's edge. Accepted for this change; the prod probe (AC12) is the external witness.
- **MAC-bound paid entitlement.** The portal authorizes the uplink's MAC. Before paying, that MAC must be shown identical after a netplan apply, a reboot and a re-provision. Nothing randomizes it by default: networkd's `MACAddressPolicy=persistent` leaves a hardware MAC alone, and wpa_supplicant defaults to `mac_addr=0`. The exception is rtw88, which falls back to a random MAC when the adapter's efuse MAC reads invalid (`efuse MAC invalid` in the kernel log). The role pins the MAC with a systemd `.link` file that matches `PermanentMACAddress`, and sets `dhcp-identifier: mac`. The MAC is also what a stranger on the site would clone to ride the paid plan, so it comes from SOPS, appears only in the file rendered on the RPi4, and is not recorded in `verification.md`. The evidence there is that it stayed identical, checked on the host.
- **[RESOLVED 2026-10-25, ADR-075 D2: stock firmware, built-in radio as the AP, 2.4 GHz] Which radio is the AP: the station cap.** Measured 2026-10-25:
  - the built-in radio runs firmware 7.45.234 (2021);
  - stock BCM43455 firmware tops out around 7 associated stations, and `max_num_sta` cannot raise it;
  - on 5 GHz only channels 36-48 are reported usable in AP mode;
  - SDIO caps throughput near 110 Mbps, far above the 3.3/1.5 Mbps uplink.

  The operator's clients (2026-10-25) are two phones, an iPad, rpi3, one or two computers, and a Garmin watch that joins briefly to sync. That is typically 5 associated stations, 6 with two computers, and 7 only if the watch syncs at that moment. An idle phone stays associated, so the count is associated devices, not devices in use. The watch also fixes the band: most Garmin watches join only 2.4 GHz networks, so the AP runs on 2.4 GHz. The options for ADR-075 are:
  1. the stock firmware, with the hard limit documented;
  2. Cypress's minimal firmware (about 19 stations), which drops 802.11k/v/r and ACS and overrides a file that package upgrades can overwrite;
  3. swapping the roles: the built-in radio becomes the uplink, with a burned-in MAC and no USB in the uplink path; the USB adapter becomes the AP, which makes the AP depend on rtw88's AP mode (unverified on 6.8) and on USB, in Spain as well.
- **The USB Wi-Fi driver is unproven for 24/7 on this kernel.** rtw88's USB support is reported rough before 6.12 (5 GHz stalls, "failed to leave lps state"); 6.8 has `rtw88_core disable_lps_deep` but no `rtw88_usb switch_usb_mode`. AC1 includes a 48-hour soak. The fallbacks are the newer raspi kernel for noble, the out-of-tree 88x2bu driver, or an adapter with a MediaTek chipset. The adapter goes on a USB 2.0 port with an extension cable, because USB 3.0 ports radiate noise into 2.4 GHz.
- **rpi3 has no screen, so the lab Wi-Fi must accept it as it is.** It joins by DHCP, with a reservation for its MAC on the old home router (`common.yaml`, SSOT-003). Reusing the home CIDR, the home router's address as the AP's, and the same reservation means rpi3 comes back on its old address even if something in its config pins it.
- **[OPEN, gates AC10] The radio may not honour two PSKs at once.** brcmfmac can offload the AP's 4-way handshake to the firmware, which holds a single key, so `wpa_psk_file` may be ignored. An on-device check decides it first: two wildcard entries, a client on the first entry's PSK. If only the last entry works, the offload is active, and `brcmfmac.feature_disable` is tried next. AC10 has a fallback that does not need the overlap (ADR-075 D8).
- **[OPEN, gates AC5] Pre-login DNS was measured with the site's own resolver.** The "DNS works before login" result came from a laptop using the resolver the site's DHCP offered. The RPi4 and Pi-hole use public resolvers. The uplink's MAC is unauthorized before the first login, so the RPi4 measures it then. If only the site's resolver answers, the login mode of ADR-075 D3 applies.
- **The radio's firmware can halt.** A weak client or an eighth station can stop the firmware, and only a reboot brings it back (raspberrypi/firmware#1403). The watchdog and liveness timer recover the cases a reboot fixes. A remote power cycle is not provided, and is recorded as debt in ADR-075.
- **Prod has no external witness while the edge is down.** Uptime Kuma shares the edge's fate, so nothing outside the fleet watches prod meanwhile. A dead-man's switch hosted outside the fleet is NOTIFY-009 (#1021), which now records this.
- **Managed work laptop.** It may enforce its own DNS or VPN and bypass Pi-hole. AC2 asks only that it gets connectivity on the lab Wi-Fi. It also shares a Wi-Fi segment with the personal devices and rpi3. Client isolation on the AP would separate them at the cost of device-to-device features (casting, AirDrop-style discovery); the real separation is the guest network (NET-004).
- **Site-identifying data in a public repository.** The lab SSID is already public: it is in git history since the early commits and in #2661's body, which cannot be undone. From now on the SSIDs live in SOPS, so the next site's names are not published; if the operator wants the Spanish flat unlocatable from wardriving databases, the lab SSID changes there and devices rejoin once.
- **Uptime Kuma reads wrong, not just absent, while the portal holds the uplink.** Before login, or after the 5-day expiry, the portal answers HTTP with its own page and HTTPS with a bad certificate. Kuma's HTTP monitors can read UP from the portal page, its HTTPS monitors alert DOWN, and no notification leaves until the uplink is back, when a stale burst arrives. The runbook's expiry section says what Kuma shows and what to ignore, and the prod probe (AC12) is the signal that counts.
- **The portal's hosts must resolve through Pi-hole.** A blocklist hit on any host the portal page loads breaks the login (AC5); the runbook names them and Pi-hole allowlists them.
- **[DECIDED 2026-10-25, operator] The PSK is now broadcast in a public building.** A captured handshake allows an offline dictionary attack. The baseline checked the reused home PSK on the host without printing it, and it falls short of a long random value. The home PSK stays only until rpi3 is reachable over the tailnet, so that every device, rpi3 included, comes back on its own. Then it is rotated within this spec (AC10). rpi3 cannot be re-keyed by hand while its only network is this Wi-Fi.
- **A hostile uplink is assumed, not just an untrusted one.** The dongle joins any open network with the site's SSID, an evil twin included. Tailscale and the devices' TLS traffic are end-to-end encrypted; Pi-hole's upstream DNS is not, and stays so: encrypted upstream DNS fails before the portal login, which is exactly when phones need DNS to detect the portal.
- **Secret handling.** The lab Wi-Fi PSK is the home network's PSK, reused so devices rejoin. It is extracted into the consuming process and never printed, and its `SecretSpec` names a real env (`SecretSpec.envs` is the audit dimension, not the file).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **AC1 — One block per site, idempotent.** Moving site changes only `networking.edge.uplink`. `make provision NODE=rpi4` converges the RPi4 to it, an immediate second run reports `changed=0`, and the RPi4's tailnet session comes up through the new uplink. A DHCP lease renewal on the uplink succeeds with the uplink's drops loaded. The Wi-Fi kind is proven live at the temporary site, and the uplink then holds for a 48-hour soak with no driver reset in the journal. The Ethernet kind is covered by a rendering test now and proven live on arrival at the next site. The temporary site has no second Ethernet port to stand in for an ISP router: the operator's only Ethernet adapter is the homelab LAN link. The hostile-uplink checks (AC3, AC7, AC9) therefore use the **uplink stand-in**: the operator's laptop runs a Wi-Fi hotspot, with its own DHCP server, and the uplink adapter joins it.
- [ ] **AC2 — The lab Wi-Fi serves the personal devices.** A client joining the lab Wi-Fi gets a lease in the lab Wi-Fi CIDR with Pi-hole as its DNS server, a domain on Pi-hole's blocklist is answered as blocked, and a public site loads. The operator's previously saved devices rejoin without re-entering credentials. rpi3 gets its reserved address, and Uptime Kuma's K3s route answers.
- [ ] **AC3 — Segmentation and uplink exposure.** From a lab Wi-Fi client with no tailnet session, a TCP connection to a homelab host's LAN address fails. From the same client with its tailnet session up, the host answers over the tailnet. (A tailnet client that accepts routes reaches the LAN through the RPi4's subnet route, which is the intended path.) From a host on the uplink side that routes the homelab and lab Wi-Fi CIDRs through the RPi4's uplink address, no connection succeeds: not to a homelab host, not to a lab Wi-Fi client, and not to SSH, Pi-hole DNS, Pi-hole web or CoreDNS on the RPi4. The scan runs over IPv4 and IPv6, from the uplink stand-in of AC1, where the operator's laptop is the uplink router and nothing isolates it. A scan from a hotel client proves nothing, because the hotel's own client isolation would give the same result. A rendering test fails if the uplink rules lose their default drop, if the forward drops in `inet kubelab` stop being complete by themselves (ufw's rules are not the forward policy), or if the role could bring the uplink up before its drops are loaded. The lab Wi-Fi CIDR is neither advertised on the tailnet nor added to any trusted-CIDR list (ADR-041's bulk trust of the tailnet must not extend to personal devices).
- [ ] **AC4 — The homelab works as before.** A host on the homelab switch gets a DHCP lease from the RPi4. With the homelab powered, its nodes reach the internet through the new uplink, the RPi4 still advertises `networking.lan_cidr` on the tailnet, staging DNS answers from a tailnet client, and `make test-e2e ENV=staging` passes.
- [ ] **AC5 — Captive portal without a screen.** On the temporary site, the portal login and the paid upgrade are completed from a client on the lab Wi-Fi, with no display or keyboard on the RPi4, and the uplink's MAC is identical across a reboot and a re-provision. The client that logs in must reach the portal through the RPi4. A laptop on the homelab switch that keeps its own hotel Wi-Fi up authorizes its own MAC, not the uplink's, so the runbook step routes it through the RPi4 first. Before that first login, the RPi4 measures whether a public resolver answers or only the site's own does, and `verification.md` records the result. If only the site's resolver answers, the login succeeds through the login mode, and leaving it restarts Pi-hole so no cached portal answer survives. Run from a laptop on the lab Wi-Fi, the edge's status target reports a portal and a dead uplink as different states. A captive uplink raises AC12's alert. This is shown live when the free class expires. The runbook covers the 5-day renewal and the paid plan's expiry.
- [ ] **AC6 — No hand-written network config.** The RPi4's network configuration is rendered by the role from the SSOT: netplan for the uplink and `eth0`, a systemd-networkd unit for the AP interface (netplan describes access points only for NetworkManager), plus the regulatory domain and the resolver. A rendering test checks that the uplink's DHCP drop-in sits in the directory of the `.network` file netplan generates for that interface, because a mismatched name silently leaves the RPi4 with no default route. Another checks that the AP interface appears in no netplan file, since a netplan-generated `.network` file for it would override the role's unit by sort order. The hand-written netplan in `docs/runbooks/rpi4-bootstrap.md` is replaced by a provisioning step. The lab Wi-Fi PSK and SSID (and any uplink PSK, SSID or BSSID) are in SOPS and `SECRET_CATALOG`, no site SSID appears in a committed file, and `make secrets-audit` passes. The resolver is a `copy` or `template`, not today's `shell` task marked `changed_when: false`, which hides a change from AC1's `changed=0`. The consumers of the RPi4's old Wi-Fi address (`wifi_lan_ip`, the `rpi4-lan` SSH alias) read the new one from the SSOT. The DHCP reservations' MAC addresses, now literals in `provision-rpi4.yml`, move to the SSOT with rpi3's.
- [ ] **AC7 — Uplink collision is reported.** An uplink lease whose address falls inside `networking.lan_cidr`, the lab Wi-Fi CIDR or the tailnet CIDR raises an operator alert from the edge. Routing survives such a lease, because the longer internal prefix wins. The address can still clash with a homelab host that dnsmasq leased it to, and the runbook names the response. A gateway inside one of those CIDRs leaves the RPi4 with no usable default route, so the edge cannot post. AC12's prod alert covers that case. Shown by a test of the detection and by live cases forced with the AC9 stand-in DHCP server handing out each kind of lease. The temporary site's `/23` makes the address case likely: the operator's laptop holds `172.16.0.123`, and nothing confines the pool to the lower half.
- [ ] **AC8 — Shaping keeps calls usable.** With the uplink shaped in both directions from its profile, neither a bulk upload nor a bulk download from a homelab host starves a lab Wi-Fi client. That client's TCP connect time under each load stays within a bound recorded in `verification.md`, measured with and without the shaper. Which direction is the download is measured on the RPi4 first.
- [ ] **AC9 — The uplink's DHCP cannot steer the edge.** A DHCP server on the uplink that offers classless static routes for an internal CIDR and its own DNS servers changes neither the RPi4's routing table nor its resolver. The test uses the uplink stand-in from AC1, with its DHCP server configured to send them.
- [ ] **AC10 — The lab Wi-Fi PSK has one source and rotates with one command, without locking anyone out.** The PSK lives only in SOPS. The AP and rpi3 both render from it, and no copy exists elsewhere. A rotation target runs these steps:
  1. It generates a random PSK of at least 24 characters into SOPS without printing it.
  2. The AP accepts the old and the new PSK during a transition window (`hostapd` `wpa_psk_file`, one `keyid=` per entry).
  3. It applies the new PSK to rpi3 first, because rpi3's only network is this Wi-Fi. rpi3 rejoins with it.
  4. A separate, explicit step retires the old PSK. It refuses to run until rpi3 answers over the tailnet on the new PSK, as its association's `keyid` shows. The AP then refuses the old PSK and accepts the new one.

  If the on-device check finds the radio offloading the handshake (see Risks), the overlap in step 2 is replaced. rpi3's saved profile is re-keyed over the tailnet while it is still associated. Then the AP switches, and rpi3 drops and rejoins within seconds. The step 4 gate stays.

  The first rotation replaces the reused home PSK once rpi3 answers over the tailnet. Entering the new PSK on unmanaged client devices is one of the declared human steps (AC11).
- [ ] **AC11 — Nothing is done by hand except the declared human steps.** The runbook names its human steps and no others: plugging in the adapters, the captive-portal login and payment, and entering the lab PSK on client devices. Every other step is a Make target backed by the toolkit or Ansible and documented in the runbook. This includes:
  - the first provisioning over the LAN (`make provision NODE=rpi4 ENV=prod BOOTSTRAP=1`, which exists);
  - the rejoin to the mesh, with a single-use, short-lived pre-auth key minted by the toolkit when the node key no longer works. Today `toolkit secrets preauth-keys` only reports and expires keys, and the tailscale role only prints instructions;
  - the operator's temporary link to the lab LAN. Today it is a NetworkManager profile created by hand on the operator's laptop on 2026-10-25 (see `verification.md`). A target that renders it from `networking.lan_cidr` replaces it.

  A read of the runbook finds no shell command outside a Make target.
- [ ] **AC12 — Prod reports an edge that cannot speak.** With the uplink cut, both by unplugging the uplink adapter and by the uplink stand-in blocking everything but DNS the way a portal does, the operator is paged through the ADR-044 fabric within a bound recorded in `verification.md`. The page arrives on a path that does not run through the edge: the phone receives it on cellular, with its Wi-Fi off. It repeats until acknowledged, because a phone still associated with a dead Wi-Fi may hold back background pushes. The page clears once the uplink is back. Stopping rpi3 while the RPi4 stays up pages the same way: a target that fails while another answers is not reported as resolved. The probe reads its targets from the SSOT, not from literals, and logs one line per target on every run, with the result as a value. A test fails if the probe's output can omit an SSOT target, or if the rules lose either the per-target alert or the alert on no lines at all (`noDataState: Alerting`).
- [ ] **AC13 — The edge recovers itself where a reboot can.** systemd's hardware watchdog is armed on the RPi4 (`RuntimeWatchdogUSec` is not zero). With the AP's driver removed to simulate a firmware halt, the liveness timer restores the lab Wi-Fi without a human step, first by reloading the driver and then by rebooting. A test checks that the timer's escalation order is rendered from the role.
