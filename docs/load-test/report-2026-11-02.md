# Load test report, 2026-11-02 (OPS-054)

**Result:** staging's ingress path served 1,000 requests per second with 0 errors in all six ramps of the final run (`7ccf57f4`). The limit it hit is Traefik's 384 MiB memory limit, not CPU: between 1,200 and 2,000 requests per second, each ramp ended with Traefik OOMKilled.

**Update, 2026-11-04:** that limit was an inherited default, not a property of the edge. With its own 1,536 MiB limit and probes that tolerate saturation, Traefik was not OOMKilled up to 3,200 requests per second offered. It ran out of CPU instead, delivering about 1,100 to 1,300/s, with memory reaching 94% of the limit (see "Past 2,000/s").

**Update, 2026-11-06:** `web` no longer serves `/` at 1,000/s. Since web#527 nginx compresses every response per request, and the pod's 500m limit now caps it near 330/s; with compression declined by the client it serves 800/s at 113m, as before (web#615, see "At the knee, by request phase"). The `api` figures and the edge's are unchanged.

Raw figures: [`2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.md`](2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.md) and [`.json`](2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.json). Method and command: [the runbook](../runbooks/load-test.md).

**Concurrency.** At 1,000/s the mean in flight was at most 4.0 requests (rate times mean latency). k6 sampled up to 383 open virtual users at that step, each with its own connection. Every failed step reached 2,400 to 4,000 virtual users, k6's ceiling of twice the rate.

**The knee is not a clean line, and that matters.** The failing rate ranged from 1,200 to 2,000/s with the same setup. p99 at healthy steps also jumps by tenfold between ramps. Stalls happen, and on a fixed arrival rate a stall makes k6 open connections to keep up. In `api` ramp 3 at 1,200/s, 1,516 virtual users took Traefik to 233 MiB and p95 to 195 ms, and it recovered. Past some connection count it does not: about 115 KiB per connection from that step, an estimate from one sample, reaches 384 MiB near 2,800. What causes the stalls was not established.

**Validity.** The generator never exceeded 34% CPU, so it was not the limit. The node stayed at 65% CPU or less with about 8.7 GB available: the failure is a container limit, not exhausted hardware. Neither `api` nor `web` restarted, and throttling was under 150 ms per step. No 429 appeared on the bypass, so it was in force.

## What did not count

- **First run, `0ee1a384`**, committed for the record. Steps went from 800 straight to 1,600, too wide to read. Its `web` ramps 2 and 3 read as failing at 50/s, but they were measured during Traefik's crash loop, not on a working service. That run is why the tool now waits for the edge to stay up 120 s between ramps.
- **The `edge mem peak` column** in `7ccf57f4` reads 384 on every row: the pod's cgroup kept the peak from the earlier OOM. Ignore it; the tool no longer reports it. Traefik's memory in the `pods` column is a sampled value.
- **Peak VUs** are sampled about once a second, so they undercount short spikes.

## Side effects

The test took staging's only ingress down repeatedly: 6 Traefik restarts in the first run, 17 in the second, each failed step about 2. Every staging route was unavailable during those restarts. Prod was not touched: only read-only `kubectl get` and `top`, no request to any prod host.

## Traefik limit after these runs

The table above was measured at 384 MiB. #2975 gave Traefik its own limit and the ramps were repeated, all on 2026-11-02 in staging.

| Limit | Run | Repeats | Highest sample | OOMKilled | Other restarts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 384 MiB | `7ccf57f4` | 3 | Limit reached | Yes | |
| 768 MiB | `f3f1ed1d` | 2 | Limit reached, by exit 137 | 2 of 4 ramps | |
| 1,536 MiB | `dbfa5158`, `4da6811b` | 1 + 3 | 973 MiB at 1,400/s | None in 8 ramps | 2 in `4da6811b`, see below (#3018) |

The highest sample is one reading per step, taken mid-step, so the true peak may be higher, and steps that restarted read low. Most of the eight ramps stopped earlier: only one reached 1,600/s and 3,200 VUs, so the claim is firmest up to 2,800 VUs. The raised limit stops the edge dying at the knee; it does not move the knee. In `4da6811b`, `api` last passed 1,000 to 1,200/s and `web` 1,200 to 1,400/s. Past that, the edge degrades by latency and errors, and at 1,600/s Traefik was restarted by its liveness probe, not by memory: the pod events read `failed liveness probe` and the last exit was 0. The run counts two restarts, 51 seconds apart, and the step data records one, at 1,600/s. The other has no step, and its cause was not read.

#3018 then relaxed Traefik's probes: liveness waits 5 s per answer and restarts only after 6 failures 15 s apart, 75 to 120 s, and readiness waits 5 s. Run `ed148e4a` on 2026-11-03 repeated the ramps from 1,400/s with `MIN_RATE=1400 MAX_RATE=2000`, 2 repeats, at the same 1,536 MiB limit.

| Probes | Run | Ramps | 1,600/s healthy | 2,000/s healthy | Traefik restarts | Highest sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chart defaults (2 s timeout, 3 failures) | `4da6811b` | 6 | | | 2, by liveness | 973 MiB |
| #3018 | `ed148e4a` | 4 | 3 of 4 | 0 of 3 (one ramp stopped at 1,600/s) | 0 | 1,328 MiB at 2,000/s |

With the edge no longer restarted, it keeps its connections open past the knee, and memory follows them: 1,328 MiB is 86% of the limit at 4,000 VUs. A ramp past 2,000/s would likely reach it. Bounding that is #3057.

### Past 2,000/s (#3057)

On 2026-11-04, at commit `0e224c0f`, two more runs went past the knee, each with one ramp per target. k6 allows twice the rate in VUs, each with a 5 s timeout, so the offered rate sets how many requests Traefik holds.

| Offered | VUs | Ramps | Achieved | Errors | Traefik CPU | Node CPU | Highest sample | Traefik restarts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,000/s (`MIN_RATE=2000`) | 4,000 | 2 | 734 to 1,128/s | 42% `api`, 94% `web` | 3.4 to 3.7 cores | 86 to 87% | 1,407 MiB | 0 |
| 3,200/s (`MIN_RATE=3200 MAX_RATE=3200`) | 6,400 | 6 | 1,149 to 1,332/s | 77 to 99.5% | 3.5 to 3.8 cores | 95 to 96% | 1,445 MiB, before a restart | 3, by liveness |

Each ramp stopped at its first step, which was already degraded, so neither run moved past the offered rate. The 2,000/s row has no raw file in the repository and cannot be checked against one: its results were replaced by the 3,200/s rerun on the same day and commit, and the figures above were read from the run's output before that. The tool now names a rerun `-2`, `-3` instead.

**The ceiling is CPU, not memory.** Offered 2,000/s or 3,200/s, the node delivered about 1,100 to 1,300/s. Traefik used 3.4 to 3.8 of its node's 4 cores, and requests past that waited until the 5 s timeout. Memory came within 6% of the 1,536 MiB limit but no run was OOMKilled. At 3,200/s Traefik was restarted three times, by liveness and not by memory: the pod events read `failed liveness probe` then `Back-off restarting failed container`, and the last exit was 0. The samples fall after the first restart, 1,445 MiB to 720 MiB, so they show memory being reset, not headroom. Whether 1,536 MiB would have held at 6,400 VUs without those restarts was not measured. Measuring it would mean changing the probe for the test.

**Why this does not change production.** In-flight requests are rate × latency. Prod's rate limiter admits 30/s per host, burst 150, so even with 5 s stalls one host holds about 300 requests, far from the 4,000 to 6,400 here. The tests reached this edge only through the limiter-bypass route. A flood spread over many hosts is CrowdSec's job, and a backend that never answers is #3076. #3057 therefore closes with no change to Traefik. `inFlightReq` would cap requests, not CPU, and the limiter already caps them.

The liveness restarts at 3,200/s are recorded, not fixed. The probes from #3018 held at 2,000/s with 4,000 VUs. At 3,200/s Traefik was past CPU saturation, `/ping` went unanswered for more than the 75 to 120 s window, and a restart followed. That is the probe answering "stuck" for an edge that could not answer anything. A wider window would only keep a failing edge longer, and prod cannot reach that load behind its limiter.

### At the knee, by request phase (2026-11-06, #3102)

Run `f3f44a24` (3 ramps from 1,000 to 2,000/s, [`2026-11-06-f3f44a24.json`](2026-11-06-f3f44a24.json)) is the first with each step split by k6's request phases. What it settles, from the `api` ramps:

- **TCP is not the stall.** `connecting` is 0 at every step, healthy or not: k6 reuses its connections, and the LAN opens new ones in no measurable time.
- **The time goes to `waiting`, then TLS amplifies it.** At each degraded step `waiting` p99 reaches 0.9 to 5 s, k6 opens connections to keep the arrival rate (VUs from 40 to 916–4,000), and each new connection pays a TLS handshake: `tls_handshaking` p99 tracks `blocked` p99 (908, 3,065 and 4,039 ms). Traefik's CPU follows the handshakes, 949m to 3,620m.
- **What starts it was open here; it is answered in the next section.** Only one of the three ramps showed a precursor: in ramp 3, `waiting` p99 had risen to 148 ms at 1,000/s, a healthy step, before the fall at 1,200/s. In ramps 1 and 2 it stayed at 9 to 59 ms until the step that fell. So the first `waiting` rise is not yet shown to come before the fall; finding what lifts it is where a GC trace or CPU profile of Traefik earns its place (#3102).

The knee moved again: 1,200, 2,000 and 1,200/s for `api`.

**`web` regressed, and the cause is isolated.** All three `web` ramps failed at 1,000/s, their first step: 395 to 410/s delivered, 72 to 85% timeouts, the `web` pod at its 500m CPU limit and throttled 82 to 86 s per 60 s step. On 2026-11-02 the same step used about 128m. A deploy landed during ramp 1 (`rollout`), so ramps 2 and 3 ran a newer image than the run's facts record. An A/B on one image (`sha-471583e`, 800/s, `--accept-encoding`) isolated it:

| `/` | `Accept-Encoding: gzip` (k6's default, and browsers') | `identity` |
|---|---|---|
| 25/s | web 112m | web 14m |
| 800/s | web at 500m, 74 s throttled, 331/s delivered, 73% timeouts | 795/s, 0 errors, p95 2.1 ms, web 113m |

Raw: [`2026-11-06-985dda03-2.json`](2026-11-06-985dda03-2.json) (gzip) and [`2026-11-06-985dda03.json`](2026-11-06-985dda03.json) (identity). One 60 s step per arm at each rate, run back to back on the same image and node; the 25/s pair is each run's baseline, below any saturation, so it is a second comparison, not a repeat of the first. The size of the gap (8x at 25/s, 0% against 73% errors at 800/s) is why one sample per arm is taken as enough; web#615's fix is to be checked with the same A/B. Since web#527 nginx deflates the same static file on every request. The fix is precompression at build, web#615. In prod every browser sends gzip, so at 500m the pod tops out near 100 to 150 requests per second on `/`.

### What starts the stall (2026-11-11, #3102)

Run `d46f3fbb` (3 ramps from 1,000 to 2,000/s, [`2026-11-11-d46f3fbb.json`](2026-11-11-d46f3fbb.json)) is the first with a timeline in each step, one sample every 2 s, from the tool merged in `dc96eb96`. Each sample holds Traefik's runtime and `websecure` figures, ace1's CPU split by cgroup, softirq and busiest core, conntrack, and ace2's CPU. The knee moved again: `api` 1,400, 1,200 and 1,600/s; `web` 1,200, 1,000 and 1,200/s. No restarts, no 429.

**Every step starts with a burst that saturates the node, healthy or not.** In the first 2 s of every step at or past 1,200/s, ace1 is at 2.7 to 4.0 of its 4 cores and the edge's mean latency is 10 to 290 ms, including the steps that pass: `api` ramp 1 at 1,200/s shows 3.99 cores and 66 ms at t = 2 s. k6 starts each step as a new process, so every connection is new and pays a TLS handshake.

**What decides the step is whether that burst settles before k6 adds virtual users.**

- A healthy step settles within 4 to 6 s: about 300 to 430 connections, then 0.5 ms and about 2 cores for the rest of the minute. Over its first 16 s, Traefik's cgroup averages 1.1 to 1.3 cores.
- A degraded step tips. Latency is still high when k6's constant-arrival-rate executor needs more virtual users to hold the rate, so it starts more. Each new user opens a connection, and the node stays at 4.0 cores. Open connections climb to `maxVUs` (twice the rate: 2,000, 2,400, 3,200), and mean latency reaches 0.6 to 3.6 s.
- Over the first 16 s of a degraded step, Traefik's cgroup averages 3.1 to 4.3 cores of the node's 3.7 to 3.9 (time-weighted; single rows disagree more, see the limits below).
- The climb ends when the pool is full. Then every step recovered, `web` included: 0.5 to 1 ms and 2.0 to 2.5 cores from t ≈ 16 to 32 s until the end, at 1,000 to 1,600/s.
- What fails the step is those first 16 to 32 s. They are enough to put p95 past 250 ms and the error rate past 1%.

**Traefik's CPU follows new connections, not requests.**

| | Requests per second | Open connections | Traefik CPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| `api` 1,400/s, t = 4 s | 439 | 1,266 (+855) | 4.63 cores |
| `api` 1,400/s, t = 8 s | 1,500 | 1,392 (+59) | 1.95 cores |
| `web` 1,200/s, t = 4 s | about 1,000 | +733 | 5.44 cores |
| `web` 1,200/s, t = 16.8 s | about 1,000 | −238 | 1.39 cores |

That is the confirming signal for handshakes, an inference: Traefik exports no handshake counter, and no CPU profile was taken (below). The signal that would rule it out is a step whose connections are opened before it starts. That step is #3294.

**Candidates ruled out, each by the signal that would have shown it:**

| Candidate | What would show it | What the degraded steps show |
|---|---|---|
| Go GC in Traefik | Cycles or stop-the-world time rising at the fall | At most 1.7 cycles/s and 5.1 ms of pause per interval in degraded steps; a healthy `web` step reached 2.5 cycles/s |
| One saturated core (softirq, NIC interrupts) | One CPU at 100% with the others idle, or softirq taking cores at the onset | All four CPUs busy (node at 4.0 cores); softirq at most 0.36 cores in the first 6 s, up to 0.93 later in the saturated phase |
| Other pods on ace1 | Node CPU outside the Traefik and target cgroups | 0.2 to 0.9 cores outside them over the first 16 s, time-weighted, the same as in the steady minute (0.6 to 0.9: k3s, the sampler) |
| conntrack | A count near `nf_conntrack_max` | At most 14,506 of 131,072 |
| The generator | ace2 over 85% CPU | At most 51% |

**So the range is the answer, and it is not request capacity.** The knee this test reports is the rate at which a cold start of `rate / 10` connections, plus the users k6 adds to hold the rate, can be absorbed by one 4-core node also terminating TLS. Whether a given step tips depends on timing within its first two seconds. That is why the same rate fails in one ramp and passes in the next, here and on 2026-11-02 to 06. With connections established, 1,000 to 1,600/s ran at 0.5 to 1 ms on about half the node. A production analogue exists: a reconnect storm after Traefik restarts or a deploy drops every client at once. #3294 separates the two numbers in the test, with a warm-up excluded from the summary.

**Limits of this measurement.**

- At 2 s resolution everything inside the first interval is simultaneous, so no order is read into t = 2 s.
- While the node is saturated, the reads stretch, and rows came 3 to 8 s apart instead of 2 s. `api` at 1,600/s lost two edge reads through the saturated apiserver.
- Per row, the cgroup figures are skewed when reads stretch. Node cores come from `/proc/stat`'s own jiffies, while cgroup cores divide by the gap between sample stamps, so a late read shifts CPU between rows: Traefik reads 5.56 cores in one row, and `other` reads 3.0 in another. Summed over a window the cgroup counters telescope, so the time-weighted figures above are the ones to quote (#3295).
- The sampler costs ace1 0.1 to 0.2 cores at rest, mostly ssh handshakes, measured as 30 s with and without it, twice (#3295). This run's knees are lower than those of 2026-11-02 to 06; with a range this wide, that cannot be attributed to the sampler.
- AC1's CPU profile was not taken. It needs pprof enabled on the staging Traefik, a change to its HelmChartConfig, and the handshake inference above is what such a profile would confirm.

## What can be published

> On a single Intel N95 mini PC running K3s (4 cores, 12 GB), a repeatable k6 test from the LAN measured 1,000 requests per second through the Traefik ingress to trivial endpoints, with 0 errors, p95 under 3 ms and p99 under 150 ms, in 60-second steps. In later runs with the rate limiter out of the path, Traefik's own 1,536 MiB memory limit and probes that tolerate saturation, the ingress delivered at most about 1,300 requests per second: Traefik used 3.5 to 3.8 of the 4 cores, and its memory reached 94% of the limit without being OOMKilled.

Until 2026-11-04 this sentence ended with Traefik OOMKilled at 384 MiB. That was a limit nobody had declared (#2975); with its own limit Traefik ran out of CPU first. The history (384 MiB, then liveness restarts, then CPU) is the "Traefik limit after these runs" section, and a page that tells it should link there.

The two halves come from different runs and configurations. The 1,000/s figures are run `7ccf57f4` (2026-11-02, Traefik at the inherited 384 MiB limit), raw file [`2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.json`](2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.json). The ceiling is run `0e224c0f` at 3,200/s offered (2026-11-04, 1,536 MiB), raw file [`2026-11-04-0e224c0f.json`](2026-11-04-0e224c0f.json). Its 2,000/s companion has no raw file, as "Past 2,000/s" says, so a page should quote the 3,200/s figures for the ceiling. Do not quote the sentence split from these two lines.

It may not be used to imply:

- **Production capacity.** It is staging, on other hardware, without the limiter. Whether and how to measure prod is #3103.
- **Application capacity.** `/health` touches no dependency and `/` is a static file. No database, auth or real page was tested.
- **Today's `web`.** The 1,000/s figure is the 2026-11-02 build. The current build compresses per request and cannot reproduce it until web#615 lands.
- **Concurrent users.** It is a request rate with a few requests in flight, from one client. There is no conversion to users.
- **Sustained load.** Each step lasted 60 seconds. There was no soak test.
- **Availability or reliability.** The test ended by crashing the edge, and the node is on demand.
- **A clean tail.** p99 at healthy steps was 146 ms at 800/s and 97.9 ms at 1,000/s, so it does not rise smoothly with load. The sentence quotes the worse of the two.
- **Exactly 1,000/s delivered.** k6 dropped up to 0.94% of scheduled arrivals at that step, so the delivered rate was about 990/s.
- **A fixed ceiling.** The failure point moved between 1,200 and 2,000/s across ramps. Past it, with Traefik's later 1,536 MiB limit, the node delivered about 1,100 to 1,300/s whatever was offered, limited by CPU (see "Past 2,000/s"). With 384 MiB, memory failed first. Why the knee moves is #3102.

## Tool changes after these runs

After the runs, review changed the tool: an edge pod replaced or down at the end of a step now counts as a restart, and cleanup removes the k6 image even if deleting the route fails. The results directory is now resolved against the repo and created, and clearing the route reports a failed `kubectl` as an error. The runs were not repeated with those changes, and the raw files record the commit they ran on.

## Defects

- #2975: Traefik's memory limit is an inherited namespace default, sized from its at-rest footprint.
- #2977: a `web` rollout timed out in-flight requests during a smoke run. Cause not yet proven.
- #3018: Traefik's liveness probe restarted the only replica under saturation.
- #3057: past 2,000/s nothing bounds the connections Traefik holds, so its memory nears the limit. Measured to 3,200/s: CPU fails first, memory reached 94% of the limit, and prod's per-host limiter keeps it far from either. Closed with no change.
- A rerun on the same day and commit replaced the earlier run's results; fixed in the change that closed #3057.
- #3076: Traefik never times out a backend's response headers.
- web#615: nginx compresses every response per request since web#527, so `/` costs 8x the CPU and the pod caps near 330/s in staging.
- #3140: the 5xx alerts matched text, not status, and paged on the load test.
