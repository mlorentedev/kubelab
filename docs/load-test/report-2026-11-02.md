# Load test report, 2026-11-02 (OPS-054)

**Result:** staging's ingress path served 1,000 requests per second with 0 errors in all six ramps of the final run (`7ccf57f4`). The limit it hit is Traefik's 384 MiB memory limit, not CPU: between 1,200 and 2,000 requests per second, each ramp ended with Traefik OOMKilled.

**Update, 2026-11-04:** that limit was an inherited default, not a property of the edge. With its own 1,536 MiB limit and probes that tolerate saturation, Traefik was not OOMKilled up to 3,200 requests per second offered. It ran out of CPU instead, delivering about 1,100 to 1,300/s, with memory reaching 94% of the limit (see "Past 2,000/s").

**Update, 2026-11-06:** `web` no longer serves `/` at 1,000/s. Since web#527 nginx compresses every response per request, and the pod's 500m limit now caps it near 330/s; with compression declined by the client it serves 800/s at 113m, as before (web#615, see "At the knee, by request phase"). The `api` figures and the edge's are unchanged.

Raw figures: [`2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.md`](2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.md) and [`.json`](2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.json). Method and command: [the runbook](../runbooks/load-test.md).

**Concurrency.** At 1,000/s the mean in flight was at most 4.0 requests (rate times mean latency). k6 sampled up to 383 open virtual users at that step, each with its own connection. Every failed step reached 2,400 to 4,000 virtual users, k6's ceiling of twice the rate.

**The knee is not a clean line, and that matters.** The failing rate ranged from 1,200 to 2,000/s with the same setup. p99 at healthy steps also jumps by tenfold between ramps. Stalls happen, and on a fixed arrival rate a stall makes k6 open connections to keep up. In `api` ramp 3 at 1,200/s, 1,516 virtual users took Traefik to 233 MiB and p95 to 195 ms, and it recovered. Past some connection count it does not: about 115 KiB per connection from that step, an estimate from one sample, reaches 384 MiB near 2,800. What causes the stalls was not established.

**Validity.** The generator never exceeded 34% CPU, so it was not the limit. The node stayed at 65% CPU or less with about 8.7 GB available: the failure is a container limit, not exhausted hardware. Neither `api` nor `web` restarted, and throttling was under 150 ms per step. No 429 appeared on the bypass, so it was in force.

## What did not count

- **First run, `0ee1a384`**, committed for the record. Steps went from 800 straight to 1,600, too wide to read. Its `web` ramps 2 and 3 read as failing at 50/s, but they were measured during Traefik's crash loop, not on a working service. That run is why the tool now waits for the edge to stay up 120 s between ramps.
- **The `edge mem peak` column** in `7ccf57f4` reads 384 on every row: the pod's cgroup kept the peak from the earlier OOM. Ignore it; the tool no longer reports it. Traefik's memory in the `pods` column is a sampled value.
- **Peak VUs** are sampled about once a second, so they undercount short spikes.

## Side effects

The test took staging's only ingress down repeatedly: 6 Traefik restarts in the first run, 17 in the second, each failed step about 2. Every staging route was unavailable during those restarts. Prod was not touched: only read-only `kubectl get` and `top`, no request to any prod host.

### Past 2,000/s (#3057)

On 2026-11-04, at commit `0e224c0f`, two more runs went past the knee, each with one ramp per target. k6 allows twice the rate in VUs, each with a 5 s timeout, so the offered rate sets how many requests Traefik holds.

| Offered | VUs | Ramps | Achieved | Errors | Traefik CPU | Node CPU | Highest sample | Traefik restarts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,000/s (`MIN_RATE=2000`) | 4,000 | 2 | 734 to 1,128/s | 42% `api`, 94% `web` | 3.4 to 3.7 cores | 86 to 87% | 1,407 MiB | 0 |
| 3,200/s (`MIN_RATE=3200 MAX_RATE=3200`) | 6,400 | 6 | 1,149 to 1,332/s | 77 to 99.5% | 3.5 to 3.8 cores | 95 to 96% | 1,445 MiB, before a restart | 3, by liveness |

Each ramp stopped at its first step, which was already degraded, so neither run moved past the offered rate. The 2,000/s row has no raw file in the repository and cannot be checked against one: its results were replaced by the 3,200/s rerun on the same day and commit, and the figures above were read from the run's output before that. The tool now names a rerun `-2`, `-3` instead.

**The ceiling is CPU, not memory.** Offered 2,000/s or 3,200/s, the node delivered about 1,100 to 1,300/s. Traefik used 3.4 to 3.8 of its node's 4 cores, and requests past that waited until the 5 s timeout. Memory came within 6% of the 1,536 MiB limit but no run was OOMKilled. At 3,200/s Traefik was restarted three times, by liveness and not by memory: the pod events read `failed liveness probe` then `Back-off restarting failed container`, and the last exit was 0. The samples fall after the first restart, 1,445 MiB to 720 MiB, so they show memory being reset, not headroom. Whether 1,536 MiB would have held at 6,400 VUs without those restarts was not measured here. It did not hold on a later build: on 2026-11-12, at 4,000 open connections, Traefik was OOMKilled at this limit ("Control on one build" below).

**Why this does not change production.** In-flight requests are rate × latency. Prod's rate limiter admits 30/s per host, burst 150, so even with 5 s stalls one host holds about 300 requests, far from the 4,000 to 6,400 here. The tests reached this edge only through the limiter-bypass route. A flood spread over many hosts is CrowdSec's job, and a backend that never answers is #3076. #3057 therefore closes with no change to Traefik. `inFlightReq` would cap requests, not CPU, and the limiter already caps them.

The liveness restarts at 3,200/s are recorded, not fixed. The probes from #3018 held at 2,000/s with 4,000 VUs. At 3,200/s Traefik was past CPU saturation, `/ping` went unanswered for more than the 75 to 120 s window, and a restart followed. That is the probe answering "stuck" for an edge that could not answer anything. A wider window would only keep a failing edge longer, and prod cannot reach that load behind its limiter.

### At the knee, by request phase (2026-11-06, #3102)

Run `f3f44a24` (3 ramps from 1,000 to 2,000/s, [`2026-11-06-f3f44a24.json`](2026-11-06-f3f44a24.json)) is the first with each step split by k6's request phases. What it settles, from the `api` ramps:

- **TCP is not the stall.** `connecting` is 0 at every step, healthy or not: k6 reuses its connections, and the LAN opens new ones in no measurable time.
- **The time goes to `waiting`, then TLS amplifies it.** At each degraded step `waiting` p99 reaches 0.9 to 5 s, k6 opens connections to keep the arrival rate (VUs from 40 to 916–4,000), and each new connection pays a TLS handshake: `tls_handshaking` p99 tracks `blocked` p99 (908, 3,065 and 4,039 ms). Traefik's CPU follows the handshakes, 949m to 3,620m.
- **What starts it was open here; it is answered in the next section.** Only one of the three ramps showed a precursor: in ramp 3, `waiting` p99 had risen to 148 ms at 1,000/s, a healthy step, before the fall at 1,200/s. In ramps 1 and 2 it stayed at 9 to 59 ms until the step that fell. So the first `waiting` rise is not yet shown to come before the fall; finding what lifts it is where a GC trace or CPU profile of Traefik earns its place (#3102).

The knee moved again: 1,200, 2,000 and 1,200/s for `api`.

**`web` regressed, and the cause is isolated.** All three `web` ramps failed at 1,000/s, their first step: 395 to 410/s delivered, 72 to 85% timeouts, the `web` pod at its 500m CPU limit and throttled 82 to 86 s per 60 s step. ### What starts the stall (2026-11-11, #3102)

Run `d46f3fbb` (3 ramps from 1,000 to 2,000/s, [`2026-11-11-d46f3fbb.json`](2026-11-11-d46f3fbb.json)) is the first with a timeline in each step, one sample every 2 s, from the tool merged in `dc96eb96`. Each sample holds Traefik's runtime and `websecure` figures, ace1's CPU split by cgroup, softirq and busiest core, conntrack, and ace2's CPU. The knee moved again: `api` 1,400, 1,200 and 1,600/s; `web` 1,200, 1,000 and 1,200/s. No restarts, no 429.

**Every step starts with a burst that saturates the node, healthy or not.** In the first 2 s of every step at or past 1,200/s, ace1 is at 2.7 to 4.0 of its 4 cores and the edge's mean latency is 10 to 290 ms, including the steps that pass: `api` ramp 1 at 1,200/s shows 3.99 cores and 66 ms at t = 2 s. k6 starts each step as a new process, so every connection is new and pays a TLS handshake.

**What decides the step is whether that burst settles before k6 adds virtual users.**

- A healthy step settles within 4 to 6 s: about 300 to 430 connections, then 0.5 ms and about 2 cores for the rest of the minute. Over its first 16 s, Traefik's cgroup averages 1.1 to 1.3 cores.
- A degraded step tips. Latency is still high when k6's constant-arrival-rate executor needs more virtual users to hold the rate, so it starts more. Each new user opens a connection, and the node stays at 4.0 cores. Open connections climb to `maxVUs` (twice the rate: 2,000, 2,400, 3,200), and mean latency reaches 0.6 to 3.6 s.
- Over the first 16 s of a degraded step, Traefik's cgroup averages 3.1 to 4.3 cores of the node's 3.7 to 3.9 (time-weighted; single rows disagree more, see the limits below).
- The climb ends when the pool is full. Then the edge's mean latency recovered in every degraded step, `web` included: 0.5 to 1 ms and 2.0 to 2.5 cores from t ≈ 16 to 32 s until the end, at 1,000 to 1,600/s. The step's own totals (achieved rate, errors, p95) still fail, because they include the burst: `web` at 1,000/s in ramp 2 reads 941/s, 0.8% errors and p95 1,281 ms, with its mean back to 0.9 ms from t = 16 s.
- What fails the step is those first 16 to 32 s. They are enough to put p95 past 250 ms and the error rate past 1%.

**Traefik's CPU follows new connections, not requests.**

| | Requests per second | Open connections | Traefik CPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| `api` 1,400/s, t = 4 s | 439 | 1,266 (+855) | 4.63 cores |
| `api` 1,400/s, t = 8 s | 1,500 | 1,392 (+59) | 1.95 cores |
| `web` 1,200/s, t = 4 s | about 1,000 | +733 | 5.44 cores |
| `web` 1,200/s, t = 16.8 s | about 1,000 | −238 | 1.39 cores |

That confirms a cost proportional to new connections, not which one. A TLS handshake is the likely candidate, but accepting a connection, its goroutines and HTTP setup also scale with new connections. Traefik exports no handshake counter, and no CPU profile was taken (below), so the attribution to TLS is an inference. The signal that would rule it out is a step whose connections are opened before it starts. That step is #3294.

**Candidates ruled out, each by the signal that would have shown it:**

| Candidate | What would show it | What the degraded steps show |
|---|---|---|
| Go GC in Traefik (stop-the-world) | Pause time rising at the fall | The pause figure is a delta of `go_gc_duration_seconds_sum`, so a pause anywhere in an interval, the first included, is counted, whatever the row's length. At most 5.1 ms of pause in any interval of a degraded step, against mean latencies of 60 ms to 3.6 s; at most 1.7 cycles/s, while a healthy `web` step reached 2.5. Concurrent mark and assist CPU is not visible (no `go_cpu_classes_*`), so GC's CPU share is not ruled out; its pauses are |
| One saturated core (softirq, NIC interrupts) | One CPU at 100% with the others idle, or softirq taking cores at the onset | All four CPUs busy (node at 4.0 cores); softirq at most 0.36 cores in the first 6 s, up to 0.93 later in the saturated phase |
| Other pods on ace1 | Node CPU outside the Traefik and target cgroups | 0.2 to 0.9 cores outside them over the first 16 s, time-weighted, the same as in the steady minute (0.6 to 0.9: k3s, the sampler) |
| conntrack | A count near `nf_conntrack_max` | At most 14,506 of 131,072 |
| The generator | ace2 over 85% CPU | At most 51% |

**So the range is the answer, and it is not warm request capacity.** The knee this test reports is the rate at which the connection setup of a cold start can be absorbed by one 4-core node: `rate / 10` connections at once, plus the users k6 adds to hold the rate. Whether a given step tips depends on timing within its first two seconds. That is why the same rate fails in one ramp and passes in the next, here and on 2026-11-02 to 06. With connections established, 1,000 to 1,600/s ran at 0.5 to 1 ms on 2.0 to 2.5 of the node's 4 cores. A production analogue exists: a reconnect storm after Traefik restarts or a deploy drops every client at once. #3294 separates the two numbers in the test, with a warm-up excluded from the summary.

**Limits of this measurement.**

- At 2 s resolution everything inside the first interval is simultaneous, so no order is read into t = 2 s.
- While the node is saturated, the reads stretch, and rows came 3 to 8 s apart instead of 2 s. `api` at 1,600/s lost two edge reads through the saturated apiserver.
- Per row, the cgroup figures are skewed when reads stretch. Node cores come from `/proc/stat`'s own jiffies, while cgroup cores divide by the gap between sample stamps, so a late read shifts CPU between rows: Traefik reads 5.56 cores in one row, and `other` reads 3.0 in another. Summed over a window the cgroup counters telescope, so the time-weighted figures above are the ones to quote. Fixed for later runs by #3295: the cgroups now divide by the kernel's own clock.
- The sampler costs ace1 0.1 to 0.2 cores at rest, mostly ssh handshakes, measured as 30 s with and without it, twice. Since #3295 the reads share one connection per node, and the cost at rest is within noise (runbook). This run's knees are lower than those of 2026-11-02 to 06; with a range this wide, that cannot be attributed to the sampler.
- AC1's CPU profile was not taken. It needs pprof enabled on the staging Traefik, a change to its HelmChartConfig, and the handshake inference above is what such a profile would confirm.

### What limits warm capacity (2026-11-12, #3308)

| Of which softirq | 16 to 19% | 21 to 25% | 55% | 60 to 61% | 73 to 93% |
| Node cores of 4 | 2.4 to 2.5 | 2.7 to 2.8 | 2.7 to 2.8 | 3.2 to 3.3 | 3.9 |
| Traefik cores | 1.3 | 1.5 to 1.8 | 2.0 | 2.4 | 2.3 to 2.4 |
| Traefik mean latency | 0.6 ms | 1 to 41 ms | 1.2 ms | 2.3 to 2.7 ms | 116 to 195 ms |
| Generator CPU | 27 to 33% | 36 to 37% | 32 to 33% | 35 to 38% | 44 to 47% |

`web`'s busiest CPU passes 95% at 2,000/s, where ramp 1 degraded on latency (p95 438 ms) and ramps 2 and 3 held with p95 36 to 46 ms. At 3,200/s it is saturated. k6 delivered 1,850 to 2,040/s there, but that shortfall is the cap: all 1,000 users were busy and k6 dropped the arrivals it had no user for. The evidence that the node queues is the latency, Traefik's own included (116 to 195 ms). `web` costs about four times the softirq per request of `api` (55% of one CPU at 1,600/s against 16 to 19% at 2,000/s), which fits its larger response: more packets per request, received and sent on one CPU. The generator stayed under 50%, so it was not the limit.

**Left open:** why the network work lands on one CPU. `cpu2` in every window points at one receive queue or one IRQ's affinity, which the sampler does not read (`/proc/interrupts`, RPS). Spreading it would be a node change, outside this test.

Limits: the cap is a test setting, not what a client does; a real burst still opens what it opens, and every cold window here degraded at 1,400/s, the ramp's first step, so this run says nothing new about the burst. The build also differed from the uncapped runs it is compared with; the next section removes that.

### Control on one build (2026-11-12, #3308)

The runs above compared a capped run with uncapped runs on older builds. Two runs on 2026-11-12, back to back at commit `bd7760cf` with the same images (`api` `sha-d5ec591`, `web` `sha-c52e509`), 3 ramps each from 1,400 to 3,200/s, differ only in the cap: [`2026-11-12-bd7760cf-uncapped.json`](2026-11-12-bd7760cf-uncapped.json) and [`2026-11-12-bd7760cf-capped.json`](2026-11-12-bd7760cf-capped.json).

| Warm knee, last healthy rate | Ramp 1 | Ramp 2 | Ramp 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `api`, k6's default pool | 2,000 | 1,600 | 1,600 |
| `api`, pool capped at 1,000 | 3,200, the highest offered | 3,200 | 3,200 |
| `web`, k6's default pool | 1,400 | 1,400 | 1,400 |
| `web`, pool capped at 1,000 | 2,000 | 2,000 | 2,000 |

The uncapped run falls within the earlier uncapped knees (`api` 1,600 to 2,000/s, `web` 1,400/s) and the capped run matches the capped one, in three of three ramps each, so the difference is the pool and not the build. Two of the uncapped steps also restarted Traefik, below.

**Traefik's memory grows with its open connections.** With 1,000 open, its highest heap sample in a warm window stayed under 300 MiB up to 2,000/s and reached 430 to 510 MiB at 3,200/s. With 3,200 open, at 1,600/s, it reached 990 to 1,150 MiB under `web` and 320 to 970 MiB under `api`. Without the cap, k6 opened 4,000 connections at 2,000/s and 4,800 at 3,200/s. Traefik was restarted twice in the uncapped run and not in the capped one. The second restart (20:19Z, `api` ramp 2 at 2,000/s) ended `OOMKilled`, exit 137, at the 1,536 MiB limit; the first one's reason was not read before its events expired. On 2026-11-04 ("Past 2,000/s") 4,000 VUs took memory to 1,407 MiB without an OOM, and at 6,400 liveness restarts reset it first. Here 4,000 connections went past the limit. #3057 closed without a change on the grounds that prod's limiter bounds requests in flight. It bounds requests per host, not open connections: idle keep-alive connections cost memory too, and prod's connection count has not been measured (see "How staging differs from production"). Whether that closure still holds is #3103's measurement to settle, not this run's.

**Where each target stands with the pool capped:**

- **`api` at 3,200/s is nearing its own pod's limit, not the node's.** No step degraded, so this is a lower bound and not a knee. The busiest CPU (`cpu2`) is at 75% busy with 21 to 23% softirq. The `api` pod used 459 to 464m of its 500m CPU limit and was throttled 4.7 to 6.0 s per 60 s step, and p95 rose from 3 to 6 ms at 2,000/s to 91 to 113 ms. The likeliest next limit is the app's CPU declaration, which a higher offered rate would have to confirm.
- **`web`'s 2,000/s is the node, not the pool.** `cpu2` is 97% busy at 2,000/s, 61 to 63% of it softirq, the same as the earlier capped run. At 3,200/s it is 99.5% busy and p50 jumps from 3 to 7 ms to 400 to 450 ms with all 1,000 users in flight: the latency is the server's, and the cap only bounds how much of the shortfall shows as dropped arrivals.
- **Without the cap, `web` falls at 1,600/s with the same CPU.** `cpu2` reads 93 to 95% at 1,600/s in both runs, healthy with 1,000 connections and degraded with 3,200. What the extra connections add is Traefik's memory (about 1 GiB against 280 MiB) on a CPU that was already near its end.

One `api` step does not fit: uncapped ramp 3 at 2,000/s had 8% errors and a 5 s p95 with the node at 2.6 cores and no restart. Its cause was not read.

## What can be published

> On a single Intel N95 mini PC running K3s (4 cores, 12 GB), a repeatable k6 test from the LAN measured 1,000 requests per second through the Traefik ingress to trivial endpoints, with 0 errors, p95 under 3 ms and p99 under 150 ms, in 60-second steps. In later runs with the rate limiter out of the path, Traefik's own 1,536 MiB memory limit and probes that tolerate saturation, the ingress delivered at most about 1,300 requests per second: Traefik used 3.5 to 3.8 of the 4 cores, and its memory reached 94% of the limit without being OOMKilled in that run.

> Judged after a 30-second warm-up, with the burst of opening connections reported apart and the load generator holding at most 1,000 connections, the same node served a trivial API endpoint at 2,000 requests per second with p95 under 6 ms and p99 under 18 ms, and stayed within the test's limits at 3,200, the highest rate offered, with p95 under 115 ms and p99 under 185 ms as the API pod reached its CPU limit. It served a static page at 1,600 requests per second with p95 under 12 ms and p99 under 60 ms, and stayed within the limits at 2,000, with p95 under 140 ms and p99 under 275 ms as one CPU of the node saturated. There were 0 errors at every one of these rates, in three of three ramps. When the generator opened a connection for every request it had in flight instead, the same build stopped at 1,600 to 2,000 and 1,400 requests per second, and at 4,000 open connections Traefik was OOMKilled at its 1,536 MiB limit.

Until 2026-11-04 this sentence ended with Traefik OOMKilled at 384 MiB. That was a limit nobody had declared (#2975); with its own limit Traefik ran out of CPU first. The history (384 MiB, then liveness restarts, then CPU) is the "Traefik limit after these runs" section, and a page that tells it should link there.

The two halves come from different runs and configurations. The 1,000/s figures are run `7ccf57f4` (2026-11-02, Traefik at the inherited 384 MiB limit), raw file [`2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.json`](2026-11-02-7ccf57f4.json). The ceiling is run `0e224c0f` at 3,200/s offered (2026-11-04, 1,536 MiB), raw file [`2026-11-04-0e224c0f.json`](2026-11-04-0e224c0f.json). Its 2,000/s companion has no raw file, as "Past 2,000/s" says, so a page should quote the 3,200/s figures for the ceiling. Do not quote the sentence split from these two lines. The warm figures are the two runs at `bd7760cf` (2026-11-12, `api` `sha-d5ec591`, `web` `sha-c52e509`), raw files [`2026-11-12-bd7760cf-capped.json`](2026-11-12-bd7760cf-capped.json) and [`2026-11-12-bd7760cf-uncapped.json`](2026-11-12-bd7760cf-uncapped.json), "Control on one build". They are the figure to quote for capacity: the cold burst gives a range, the warm window a line. Quote them with the warm-up, never as the rate a cold client reaches, and the capped figure with its cap and the uncapped one beside it, never one alone. The static page's 2,000/s is "within the limits", not a clean figure: the earlier capped run on another build (`6fd47188`) degraded there in one ramp of three, so it held in five ramps of six. Until 2026-11-12 the sentence quoted run `b33d4c96` (1,600 and 1,400, uncapped).

It may not be used to imply:

- **Production capacity.** It is staging, on other hardware, without the limiter. Whether and how to measure prod is #3103.
- **Application capacity.** `/health` touches no dependency and `/` is a static file. No database, auth or real page was tested.
- **Today's `web`, from the 1,000/s figure.** That figure is the 2026-11-02 build. Builds between web#527 and web#616 (2026-11-06) compressed per request and capped near 330/s. The warm figure's build, `sha-c52e509`, includes web#616.
- **Concurrent users.** It is a request rate with a few requests in flight, from one client. There is no conversion to users.
- **Sustained load.** Each step lasted 60 seconds. There was no soak test.
- **Availability or reliability.** The test ended by crashing the edge, and the node is on demand.
- **A clean tail.** p99 at healthy steps was 146 ms at 800/s and 97.9 ms at 1,000/s, so it does not rise smoothly with load. The sentence quotes the worse of the two.
- **Exactly 1,000/s delivered.** k6 dropped up to 0.94% of scheduled arrivals at that step, so the delivered rate was about 990/s.
- **A fixed ceiling.** The failure point moved between 1,200 and 2,000/s across ramps. Past it, with Traefik's later 1,536 MiB limit, the node delivered about 1,100 to 1,300/s whatever was offered, limited by CPU (see "Past 2,000/s"). With 384 MiB, memory failed first. Why the knee moves is #3102. Since #3294 the test reports a warm knee apart from the cold burst, and with k6's default pool it does not move: 1,600/s for `api` and 1,400/s for `web` in three of three ramps ("Warm capacity apart from the cold burst"). It is the second quoted sentence. With the pool capped at 1,000 it moves up ("What limits warm capacity").

## Tool changes after these runs

After the runs, review changed the tool: an edge pod replaced or down at the end of a step now counts as a restart, and cleanup removes the k6 image even if deleting the route fails. The results directory is now resolved against the repo and created, and clearing the route reports a failed `kubectl` as an error. The runs were not repeated with those changes, and the raw files record the commit they ran on.

## Defects

- #2975: Traefik's memory limit is an inherited namespace default, sized from its at-rest footprint.
- #2977: a `web` rollout timed out in-flight requests during a smoke run. Cause not yet proven.
- #3018: Traefik's liveness probe restarted the only replica under saturation.
- #3057: past 2,000/s nothing bounds the connections Traefik holds, so its memory nears the limit. Measured to 3,200/s: CPU fails first, memory reached 94% of the limit, and prod's per-host limiter keeps it far from either. Closed with no change.
- A rerun on the same day and commit replaced the earlier run's results; fixed in the change that closed #3057.
- #3076: Traefik never times out a backend's response headers.
- web#615 (fixed by web#616, 2026-11-06): nginx compressed every response per request since web#527, so `/` cost 8x the CPU and the pod capped near 330/s in staging.
- #3140: the 5xx alerts matched text, not status, and paged on the load test.
